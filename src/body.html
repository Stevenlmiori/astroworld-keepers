Astroworld Keepers

Every player left on your final 2025 roster costs one round earlier than where he went last year. Every player is rated 0–100 on how much he actually wins you: the fantasy points gained by keeping him instead of drafting at that pick, measured against whoever would really still be on the board.

How the score works

A keeper is only worth what he beats. Ratings come from the projected fantasy points you gain by keeping a player rather than spending that pick — valued in this league's exact scoring and lineup.

Value above replacement, by position

Every player is worth what he scores above the last startable player at his position — the guy you could get off waivers for free. That baseline is set by the lineup: three WR slots make receivers deep, one QB slot makes quarterbacks nearly worthless past the elite few.

What you'd get instead

The old version compared a player's rank to his pick number. That badly overrates keepers at the top of the draft, because pick 13 still lands an excellent player. So instead the model simulates the board: it removes all twelve kept players, accounts for the picks those keepers consume, and reads off who is actually still there.

This is why FP rank and pick slot should never be read against each other. Jonathan Taylor is ranked 11th and costs pick 10, which looks like paying full price — but he is RB4, and the best player left at pick 10 is a WR9. With three WR starting slots and two RB, receivers run far deeper, so the ninth-best WR is much easier to replace than the fourth-best RB. Same neighbourhood on the consensus board, forty-four points apart in what they actually win you.

points gained = value(your keeper) − value(best player left at that pick)

Those points are then stretched onto a 0–100 rating, so the number is the grade: 90s are an A, 80s a B, 70s a C, 60s a D, and anything in the 50s or below is an F. 100 is reserved for a keeper nobody in this league has — an elite player at a near-free pick.

Where this is soft

Late picks are valued at zero, which flatters deep keepers — real late picks are lottery tickets. Projections can't price suspensions or injuries.

Four of the twelve teams are a genuine tie: re-running the whole model under different assumptions about how deep startable players run at each position keeps swapping which of their top two comes out ahead. Those teams are called out on the cards at the bottom of the page. Treat the pair as level and decide on gut, or on who you trust to stay healthy.

Locked out

Disqualified before the math runs — 2025 first-rounders, and players already kept once.
